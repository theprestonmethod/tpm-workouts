CIM NEUROMOTOR SCREEN
Enable to reveal the full neuromotor battery. For neuro-support clients, also select a condition below.
Test 1 — Single Leg Balance with Dual Task

Client balances on one leg while performing a simultaneous cognitive task. Record seconds to balance break. Dual Task Cost % auto-calculates using the standard SLB scores from Section 4.

Test 2A — Wall Bounce

Client throws a tennis ball against the wall from a fixed distance, allowing it to bounce on the floor before catching — dominant hand throws, non-dominant catches, then reversed. Coach observes force calibration speed and cross-body coordination quality. Tests predictive motor control, force modulation, and interhemispheric coordination.

SCORING GUIDE — 3 = Performs with ease  ·  2 = Performs with minor difficulty  ·  1 = Significant difficulty or inconsistency  ·  0 = Unable to perform
Test 2A Score: — / 6
Test 2B — Walking Toss with Conversation

Client walks a set path while tossing and catching a tennis ball. Coach cues "eyes on the ball" at the start and reintroduces naturally during the test. Conversation is introduced organically mid-walk. Coach observes gait changes, visual dependency, cognitive-motor interference, and triple-task capacity. This is a dual and triple task locomotion test assessing motor-cognitive interference — a validated marker of fall risk and neurological decline.

SCORING GUIDE — 3 = Performs with ease  ·  2 = Minor difficulty  ·  1 = Significant difficulty  ·  0 = Unable to perform / unsafe
Test 2B Score: — / 12 Combined Test 2 Score: — / 18
Test 3 — Movement Sequence Learning

Coach demonstrates a sequence of movements. Client repeats from memory. Tests motor learning and sequential pattern acquisition.

Test 4 — Movement Memory

Coach teaches 3–4 movements, pauses 2–3 minutes (client performs a different task), then asks for recall. Tests motor memory consolidation.

0 = no recall  ·  1 = partial  ·  2 = full with cueing  ·  3 = full independent

Test 5 — Short-Term Recall Under Load

Client given a short list of items to remember, then performs a physical task. Coach asks for recall immediately after. Tests working memory under physical stress.

Test 6 — Contralateral Rhythmic Sequencing on Balance Pad

Client stands on a foam balance pad with feet hip-width apart. Coach demonstrates a contralateral movement pattern — right arm forward, left leg back — and establishes a rhythm verbally or with a hand clap. Test progresses through three layers of complexity. Layer 1: client performs pattern at their own pace. Layer 2: client matches a coach-set rhythm. Layer 3: coach calls "switch" randomly to reverse the pattern mid-sequence. Coach observes for rhythm degradation, motor overflow into the uninstructed limb, and adaptation speed. This test assesses interhemispheric coordination, motor inhibition, and rhythmic pattern generation — capacities that decline early in neurological and chronic disease populations.

EQUIPMENT — Foam balance pad  ·  SCORING — 3 = Performs with ease  ·  2 = Minor difficulty  ·  1 = Significant difficulty  ·  0 = Unable to perform

Parkinson's — Medication State at Assessment · Record before testing

Motor performance varies significantly between ON and OFF medication states. Always record this before any motor testing — it is essential for longitudinal data validity and case study documentation.

Parkinson's — Timed Up and Go · Coach-administered

Gold standard mobility test for PD. Normal <12 sec; >20 sec indicates high fall risk. The dual task cost % is a key neurological marker — high cost (>20%) indicates basal ganglia automaticity impairment.

Parkinson's — Fine Motor & Dexterity · Coach-administered

Dexterity tasks have the highest sensitivity for distinguishing early PD from healthy controls (effect size 0.52–0.63). Asymmetry between hands is a primary early indicator.

Finger Tapping
Rapid Alternating Forearm Movements — supinate/pronate as fast as possible for 10 sec
Handwriting Assessment — client writes "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog" in their normal handwriting
Spiral Drawing — client traces or draws a spiral freehand; observe for tremor, line deviation, size reduction
3 = Clean · 2 = Minor tremor · 1 = Significant deviation · 0 = Unable
Parkinson's — Gait & Movement Analysis · Coach-administered

Observe the client walking at natural pace, then walking while talking. Note all changes between single and dual task conditions. Arm swing asymmetry is among the earliest observable PD markers.

Arm Swing

MS — Visual & Sensory Pre-Screen · Record before balance testing

Visual changes and proprioceptive deficits directly affect balance test validity. Document before any balance or gait testing — required for accurate interpretation of results.

MS — Timed Walk Tests · Coach-administered

The T25FW is a primary MS research outcome measure. The 10MWT at comfortable and fast speed captures gait speed reserve — the difference between the two is a sensitive marker of neurological reserve and fall risk.

Timed 25-Foot Walk (T25FW)
10-Metre Walk Test (10MWT)
MS — Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) · Coach-administered

SPPB is validated for detecting early functional decline in MS, including in high-functioning clients. Three components: standing balance progression, gait speed, and 5× sit-to-stand. Each scored 0–4; total 12.

Standing Balance Progression — hold each 10 sec
5× Sit-to-Stand
MS — Coordination & Motor Control Screen · Coach-administered

MS produces intention tremor (tremor on movement, absent at rest) — distinct from PD's resting tremor. Coordination deficits often present asymmetrically and reflect cerebellar involvement.

Rapid Alternating Forearm Movements — supinate/pronate as fast as possible for 10 sec
Stepping in Place — march in place 60 sec, observe symmetry and rhythm
Intention Tremor Observation
MS — Proprioceptive & Sensory Screen · Coach-administered

Sensory reweighting deficits are a hallmark of MS. Balance significantly worsens when visual input is removed, revealing somatosensory and vestibular dependence. These times compare to CIM Test 1 (eyes open) for sensory contribution analysis.

MS — Fatigue, Heat Sensitivity & Recovery

MS fatigue follows a characteristic rapid-acceleration pattern distinct from deconditioning fatigue. Tracking RPE at three points captures this curve. Uhthoff's phenomenon (heat-induced symptom worsening) affects 60–80% of PwMS and directly impacts session design.

MS — Fine Motor & Spasticity · observational
Refer to clinician: EDSS scoring, 9-Hole Peg Test (formal administration), clinical spasticity grading (Modified Ashworth Scale), BICAMS cognitive battery — flag for the client's neurologist or MS clinic.
